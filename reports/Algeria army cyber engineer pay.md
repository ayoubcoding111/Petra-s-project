# Army cyber pay wins early, then trails

Algeria publishes no pay grid for its officers, so every figure for a military cybersecurity engineer comes from job and salary sites that cite no decree and no payslip. Taken together, those sites put a newly commissioned officer at about **70,000–100,000 DZD a month**. That is about €465–660 at the official rate, or €255–360 at the street rate. Pay rises to about **85,000–150,000 DZD as commandant** and **140,000–220,000 DZD as colonel**. No law, decree or credible report shows extra pay for engineers or cyber specialists, so assume you would get the same rank-based pay as any other officer. The only MDN pay you can calculate from official texts is the civilian PCA route. There, an ingénieur d'État or Master in informatique is placed in category 13, with a **base of 35,010 DZD** and about **49,000–59,500 DZD gross** once the standard engineer allowances are added. That is roughly half to three-quarters of an officer's reported pay. The army's strongest financial argument is the package around the salary. It includes free military healthcare for you and your family for life, an **immediate pension after 25 years** worth 3% of pay per year served, and housing help that has included **2,000,000 DZD interest-free loans**. The price is that the army controls your earnings. The statute bans any private paid work, so freelancing is out. You must serve **twice the length of your training** or repay its full cost, including the pay you received while training. The army can also refuse your resignation until you reach your rank's age or service limit. Compared with civilian jobs, a new officer out-earns most entry-level public and private cyber posts (about 45,000–90,000 DZD). Within five to ten years, though, private security managers report **150,000–300,000 DZD**, and freelancers with foreign clients report **€1,000–3,000 a month**, and the army rules that work out.

Arabic salary sites use different rank names from French ones. **ملازم is sous-lieutenant and ملازم أول is lieutenant.** JOBSdz's French and Arabic versions of the same article pair them this way, with ~90,000 and ~98,000 DZD respectively ([JOBSdz AR, Sept 2025](https://jobsdz.com/ar/2025/09/%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84-2025-%D8%B3%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%B7%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF/)). This matters because the only documented "degree premium" is a rank. An unofficial 2023 source says a Licence holder recruited on their degree is commissioned **sous-lieutenant**, while a Master or Ingénieur holder starts one rank higher as **lieutenant** ([Defense-Arab, Aug 2023](https://defense-arab.com/news/76330/)). On the job-site figures, that higher first rank is worth about **4,000–8,000 DZD a month** at the start of your career.

The table leaves several things out. No source separates base pay (the *solde*) from allowances, or gross pay from net. None of the figures clearly includes a southern-posting or risk allowance, and those allowances can be large (see below). Rank and seniority are the main drivers of pay, but no source gives the size of the seniority steps within a rank.

## Your entry route decides which row you land on

The [earlier report on entry routes](Algeria%20military%20entry%20for%20CS%20students.md) covers the routes into the MDN in detail. For pay, the key point is that each route puts you on a different pay system. The short version follows.

| Route | Who can use it (age on 31 December) | What you are paid as |
|---|---|---|
| Officer cadet recruited on your degree ("sur titre"): land, air, naval and air-defence forces, Gendarmerie, Garde Républicaine | Licence ≤22, Master/Ingénieur ≤24; single; pre-registration opened 2 Aug 2026 | Cadet pay (amount unpublished) for about 2 years, then officer pay from sous-lieutenant or lieutenant |
| Military schools (Cherchell informatique, EST Koléa, ENPEI then EMP) | Only that year's bac holders aged 18–21, so closed to university students | Cadet pay for 4–5 years, then officer pay |
| Contract NCO or rank-and-file soldier | Ages 18–22; a degree earns no higher rank | NCO or soldier pay (~48,000–68,000) |
| Civilian PCA post | Ingénieur d'État/Master, DEUA, TS or technicien in informatique; age caps of 35/40 in 2022; national service settled; resident in the post's wilaya | Civil-service grid; category 13 for engineers |
| EMP competition for Master holders | Master in informatique; no dates or age limit published | Unknown; probably a doctoral intake |
| National service | Men from 19, for 12 months | Conscript pay (~10,000–25,000); an obligation, not a career |

The sources for the routes are the 2026 recruitment guide and sur-titre announcement ([Algerie360, 3 Jul 2026](https://www.algerie360.com/recrutement-anp-2026-le-mdn-publie-le-guide-dacces-aux-ecoles-militaires/); [Algerie360, 2 Aug 2026](https://www.algerie360.com/mdn-lance-le-recrutement-dofficiers-de-carriere-sur-titre-comment-sinscrire-en-ligne/)), the Gendarmerie's officer page ([MDN Gendarmerie](https://www.mdn.dz/site_cgn/sommaire/form_rec/recrutement/officiers/off_fr.php)), the 2025 PCA campaign ([Algerie360, 6 Feb 2025](https://www.algerie360.com/le-mdn-recrute-des-personnels-civils-assimiles-modalites-et-documents-requis/)) and the national-service law ([Loi 14-06, JO n°48/2014](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/dz/2014/dz-government-gazette-dated-2014-08-10-no-48.pdf)). One rule links two routes: **24 months as a career soldier counts in place of national service** (Art. 60 of Loi 14-06). The earlier report could not find the minimum length of service or the training-repayment rules. This research found both in the military statute, and they are covered in the lock-in section below.

## No pay grid is published, so the numbers come from job sites that copy each other

The legal structure is clear even though the amounts are not. Article 52 of **Ordonnance 06-02 of 28 February 2006**, the general statute of military personnel, splits an officer's pay into two parts. The first is a *solde* set by rank and by seniority in rank or in service. The second is a set of *indemnités* (allowances) tied to the type of job, its demands, the posting and its risks. Officers can also receive benefits in kind. The same article adds that any general measure affecting civil servants' pay "est, sous réserve des adaptations nécessaires, étendue aux personnels militaires" ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). What is missing is the decree that turns this structure into numbers. A legal summary of the statute's pay articles names no implementing decree ([Legal Doctrine](https://legal-doctrine.com/edition/le-statut-g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral-des-militaires)). Searches in French and Arabic for a military pay grid or allowance scheme found only civil-service texts, and mdn.dz could not be reached during the research. A 2026 decree on seconding soldiers to civilian administrations says they stay paid under "the regulations in force within the Ministry of National Defence". That confirms the MDN runs its own pay system ([Dzair Tube, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.dzair-tube.dz/en/conditions-for-detachment-of-military-personnel-to-civil-public-administrations-set-forth/)). The most likely explanation is that the military grid exists but is not published. That is an inference from its absence, not a confirmed fact.

The figures therefore come from salary and job sites, and they fall into two groups. The **older group** traces back to about 2022–2023 and is still recycled under new dates. Defense-Arab gave **73,000 DZD for a sous-lieutenant and 77,000 for a lieutenant** in August 2023 ([Defense-Arab](https://defense-arab.com/news/76330/)). hmsalaries repeated the same series (73k, 77k, 84k for capitaine, 89k for commandant), and its page now returns 404 ([hmsalaries](https://hmsalaries.com/salary-of-an-officer-in-the-algerian-army/)). Dzayerinfo's article dated July 2026 gives 69k, 72k, 79k and 83k for the same four ranks, then 120k for lieutenant-colonel and 140k for colonel, and its own text contradicts its table ([dzayerinfo, Jul 2026](https://dzayerinfo.com/2026/07/27/%D8%B3%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%A3%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%8A%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%B5%D9%8A/)). The **newer group** is a single source. JOBSdz's September 2025 guide gives **about 90,000 DZD net for a sous-lieutenant, about 98,000 for a lieutenant, over 115,000 for a capitaine and 130,000–150,000 for a commandant**. It gives over 180,000 for lieutenant-colonels and colonels, "possibly over 220,000" ([JOBSdz, Sept 2025](https://jobsdz.com/fr/2025/09/salaires-en-algerie-police-gendarmerie-armee-grille-complete-2025/)). It cites no decree or payslip. Two outliers should be ignored: one site puts a sous-lieutenant at 150,000–170,000 DZD ([hmsalaries ranks](https://hmsalaries.com/ranks-of-the-algerian-army/)), and another puts a Garde Républicaine sous-lieutenant at 90,000–100,000 ([City-Salary](https://city-salary.com/%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%85%D9%87%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1%D9%8A/)).

The newer group is the better guide to 2026 pay, for a reason the sites themselves do not give. Civil-service pay rose sharply after 2021. Category 13 base pay, the level of a state engineer, went from **26,010 DZD (2008–2021) to 35,010 DZD in 2024, a rise of 34.6%**, through decrees 22-138 and 23-54 ([JO n°03, 2023](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2023/F2023003.pdf); [Décret 22-138](https://univ-bejaia.dz/doc/personnel/Decret-presidentiel-22-138.pdf)). Article 52's extension clause means the military should have received comparable raises. The jump in the job-site figures, from about 73,000 to about 90,000 DZD for a sous-lieutenant (+23%), is the size you would expect. Still, no MDN communiqué, El Djeich article or Journal officiel text confirming a military raise in 2022–2026 was found. The two groups also tell different career stories. In the older figures, each junior promotion adds only 4,000–7,000 DZD. In the 2025 figures, promotions add about 8,000 DZD at first and 17,000 DZD or more later.

Four gaps matter for your decision. First, **cadet pay is unknown**. The statute requires early leavers to repay "les traitements perçus" during training, which proves cadets are paid, but no amount was found ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). Second, **posting changes pay a lot, by unofficial accounts**. JOBSdz says the extreme-South zone allowance can "presque doubler" base pay and puts a contract soldier at about 65,000 DZD in the South or in elite units, against 48,000 elsewhere ([JOBSdz, Sept 2025](https://jobsdz.com/fr/2025/09/salaires-en-algerie-police-gendarmerie-armee-grille-complete-2025/)). The civil-service version of the southern allowance is still calculated on a 1989 base salary, which unions disputed in 2022 ([Maroc Diplomatique, 2022](https://maroc-diplomatique.net/fonction-publique-greve-generale-et-rassemblements/)). Third, **family allowances barely count**: the national rate is 600 DZD per child, and the labour minister said it has not been raised since 1997 ([Algerie360](https://www.algerie360.com/allocations-familiales-algerie-conditions-cnas/); [Echaab](https://www.echaab.dz/?p=143406)). Fourth, **the next raise is pending**. In October 2026 President Tebboune repeated his pledge to raise salaries from 2027, without mentioning the army ([APS](https://www.aps.dz/fr/presidence-news/mut10xq6-le-president-de-la-republique-reitere-son-engagement-a-revaloriser-les-salaires-en-2027)). Article 52 should carry that raise over. Uniformed corps can also get their own reviews, as the Protection civile did in September 2026 ([La Sentinelle, 22 Sept 2026](https://lasentinelle.dz/index.php/2026/09/22/revalorisation-generale-des-salaires-de-la-protection-civile-les-personnels-affichent-leur-satisfecit/)). The only way to replace these estimates with a real number is a recent payslip (*fiche de solde*) for a newly commissioned officer in transmissions or informatique.

## Nothing on paper pays a cyber officer more than any other officer

Article 52 leaves room for a technical allowance, but nothing fills that room. No published decree gives an amount or rate for a qualification, technical, specialty, hardship, risk or performance allowance for military engineers. None mentions any signals, electronic-warfare or cyber allowance either ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). One trap to avoid: a 40% "indemnité de qualification" published in January 2025 belongs to decree 25-55 for **education-sector** staff. It has nothing to do with the army ([JO n°04, 23 Jan 2025](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2025/F2025004.pdf)).

The salary sites do not fill the gap either. City-Salary gives a "military engineer" 70,000–140,000 DZD. By specialty, it gives 85,000–135,000 for telecommunications, 80,000–130,000 for electrical, 75,000–125,000 for mechanical, 70,000–120,000 for civil and up to 140,000 for aviation. It does not list informatique or cyber at all. It names housing, transport, risk and mission allowances but gives no amounts and no source ([City-Salary, Jan 2025](https://city-salary.com/%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D9%85%D9%87%D9%86%D8%AF%D8%B3-%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1/)). These ranges look like editorial estimates, not data. JOBSdz says entry pay is "particulièrement compétitif … surtout pour les spécialités techniques et rares", but it gives no premium amount ([JOBSdz, Sept 2025](https://jobsdz.com/fr/2025/09/salaires-en-algerie-police-gendarmerie-armee-grille-complete-2025/)).

The closest real model is civilian. In the civil service's common corps, engineers in the informatique track receive an **"indemnité des services techniques communs" of 40% of base pay**, against 25% for technicians. They also receive a **performance bonus of 0–30%**, paid quarterly ([Algerie360, 1 Jun 2010](https://www.algerie360.com/fonction-publique-trois-nouveaux-regimes-indemnitaires-promulgues/)). If the army pays engineers something similar, the premium would be meaningful. That is a hypothesis, not evidence.

The army's cyber agency does not change the picture. Presidential decree 20-05 of January 2020 makes the **Agence de sécurité des systèmes d'information (ASSI)** a public administrative body of the MDN. Its steering committee deliberates on "les rémunérations des personnels de l'agence", and Article 39 subjects the agency to all MDN rules ([JO n°04, 26 Jan 2020](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2020/F2020004.pdf)). No later decree on ASSI pay was found. Officers there are paid like other officers, and civilians fall under the PCA statute described in the next section ([JO n°22, 2023](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/dz/2023/dz-government-gazette-dated-2023-04-04-no-22.pdf)). The research also found no cyber retention bonus like those some Western militaries pay. When you decide, assume no cyber premium, and treat any technical allowance as a bonus you cannot count on. The levers you can document are rank, seniority and posting.

## The civilian PCA route pays about 60% of an officer's salary, on a grid you can check

**Presidential decree 23-143 of 27 March 2023** is the statute for the MDN's civilian staff. It creates two groups: PCA (*personnels civils assimilés*), who are permanent, and contract civilians. Three of its articles set out how they are paid. Article 5 extends to them every measure affecting civil servants' "rémunération … et déroulement de carrière", "sous réserve des adaptations nécessaires". Articles 28 and 34 leave pay, allowances and grids to "la réglementation en vigueur au sein du MDN", which is not published. Article 8 says the classification of grades "s'inspire" of national rules ([JO n°22, 4 Apr 2023](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/dz/2023/dz-government-gazette-dated-2023-04-04-no-22.pdf)). So the best estimate is the civil-service grid. In the civil service, an **ingénieur d'État en informatique is category 13**, an ingénieur principal category 14 and an ingénieur en chef category 16 ([JO n°03, 2008, décret 08-04](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2008/F2008003.pdf)). The February 2025 PCA campaign listed "Ingénieur d'État/Master informatique" as a single post, so a Master holder almost certainly enters at the same level ([a-onec, Feb 2025](https://www.a-onec.com/2025/02/mdn-dz-concours.html)).

The arithmetic uses official numbers. Base pay equals the category's minimum index plus your seniority-step index, multiplied by a **point value of 45 DZD**. There are 12 seniority steps (*échelons*), each taking 2.5–3.5 years ([JO n°61, 2007, décret 07-304](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2007/F2007061.pdf)). Since 1 January 2024, category 13 starts at **index 778**, which gives a **base of 35,010 DZD**. The 12th step adds 467 points, for a base of 56,025 DZD ([JO n°03, 2023](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2023/F2023003.pdf)). The CarrierePlus-DZ simulator shows the same 35,010 DZD base for category-13 engineers. It estimates an initial net of **45,910–48,235 DZD**, without showing how it calculates this ([CarrierePlus-DZ, grade 410](https://dzcarriereplus.com/grade/410/); [grade 722](https://dzcarriereplus.com/grade/722/)).

| PCA engineer, category 13, step 0 (2024 grid, still current) | DZD/month |
|---|---|
| Base pay (778 × 45) | 35,010 |
| + Technical allowance, 40% (civil-service rule for informatique engineers) | 14,004 |
| = Guaranteed gross | ~49,000 |
| + Performance bonus, 0–30% (paid quarterly, shown per month) | 0–10,503 |
| = Gross range | **~49,000–59,500** |
| Simulator's initial net (unofficial) | ~45,900–48,200 |

This assumes the MDN copies the civil-service allowances, which Article 5 of 23-143 suggests but no MDN text confirms. No grid change has been published for 2025 or 2026. The only 2026 pay measure is the SNMG rise from 20,000 to 24,000 DZD ([APS, 30 Nov 2025](https://www.aps.dz/fr/presidence-news/mim6ow3o-)). The civilian grid is also **flat**. Category 14 starts at 36,945 DZD and category 16 at 41,085 DZD, so moving from ingénieur d'État to ingénieur en chef adds only about 6,000 DZD of base pay ([JO n°03, 2023](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2023/F2023003.pdf)). Article 33 of 23-143 lets some top-level grades be classed "hors catégorie" because of the qualifications they require, but nothing shows this applies to informatique ([JO n°22, 2023](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/dz/2023/dz-government-gazette-dated-2023-04-04-no-22.pdf)). If you have worked in the public sector before, the grid credits **1.4% of base pay per year** of earlier public-sector service, and 0.7% per year of other experience ([JO n°61, 2007](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2007/F2007061.pdf)).

On these numbers, a new lieutenant (77,000–98,000 DZD on unofficial figures) earns about **1.3–2 times a starting PCA engineer**. PCA also pays less than other state employers. In July 2024 the Haut Commissariat à la Numérisation, attached to the Presidency, offered **74,000 DZD base** to new ingénieurs d'État in computing with no experience ([Algerie360, 2 Jul 2024](https://www.algerie360.com/avec-un-salaire-de-74-000-da-le-hcn-recrute-les-titulaires-de-ce-diplome/)). PCA's case rests on access and conditions, not pay. The sources mention no single-status rule, there is no military training, and the 2022 age caps were 35 and 40. PCA staff, retirees and their dependants get free care in military health facilities (Art. 23). They get 2.5 days of leave per month up to 30 days (Art. 42), plus extra leave in southern wilayas (Art. 45). They also receive family allowances (Art. 28) ([JO n°22, 2023](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/dz/2023/dz-government-gazette-dated-2023-04-04-no-22.pdf)).

## Healthcare, an early pension and housing loans are the real premium

Healthcare is the benefit most clearly written into law. Article 55 of the statute gives serving officers, **retired officers and their dependants** access to free care in military hospitals ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). In 2012 the MDN's social services signed agreements with private clinics in Algiers, Chlef, Oran and Annaba. Under them, serving and contract personnel, MDN civilians and their families were treated across specialties, with **100% of costs** paid by the military social-security fund ([Echorouk, 2012](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D9%85%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1-%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AF)). The research did not confirm that the arrangement still runs. The network is still growing, though: a social medical centre and a kindergarten opened in Aïn Témouchent in April 2026 ([Echorouk, 25 Apr 2026](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%B3%D9%83%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC)). No one has put a cash value on lifetime family healthcare.

The pension is the benefit with the clearest numbers. Under the military pensions code (Ordonnance 76-106, amended in 2021), you need **15 years** of service for any pension. Officers receive an **immediate pension after 25 years**, or earlier if they are discharged on reaching their rank's age limit, when their post is abolished, or for a service-related illness. The pension accrues at **3% per year of service, capped at 90%**. It is calculated on gross pay for your rank and index, **including the highest allowances received in the last five years**. It cannot fall below the SNMG. A surviving spouse receives 70% (the source text is partly garbled), each minor orphan 10%, and a death grant equals 12 months of pension ([Code des pensions militaires, ORA Constantine](https://oraconstantine.com/laws/%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9.php)). The 2021 amendment kept the 15-year minimum and set an **8-year minimum for an officer's separation grant** ([Radio Algérie, 20 Apr 2021](https://radioalgerie.dz/news/ar/article/20210420/210380.html)).

Here is an illustration, using the job-site pay figures and assuming your cadet years count as service. A Master holder who joins at 24 and serves 25 years leaves at about **49 with 75% of reference pay**. If that officer retires as a commandant on 130,000–150,000 DZD, the pension is about **97,500–112,500 DZD a month for life**, at an age when a civilian engineer is mid-career. Serving 30 years raises the rate to 90%.

Rank limits shape this career path. Officers who are not promoted in time are retired. As reported for the 2021 amendment, the limits are 34 years of age or 15 years of service for a sous-lieutenant, 37/18 for a lieutenant, 42/22 for a capitaine, 45/25 for a commandant, 48/28 for a lieutenant-colonel and 53/32 for a colonel. Technical corps get three extra years ([NN-Algeria, 5 Jun 2021](https://nn-algeria.dz/%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%88%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE/)). The 2006 text had tighter limits, and one legal summary still cites them ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf); [Legal Doctrine](https://legal-doctrine.com/edition/le-statut-g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral-des-militaires)). A career that stalls at capitaine or commandant therefore ends early. Because discharge at the age limit triggers an immediate pension, it does not end with nothing.

Housing is probably the largest single non-cash benefit. Under a 2019 MDN savings scheme, **officers (and assimilated civilians)** could get an **interest-free loan of 2,000,000 DZD over 144 months plus a one-off subsidy of 1,000,000 DZD**. NCOs and civilians in grades 11–17 could get a 1,000,000 DZD loan and a 1,200,000 DZD subsidy ([Echorouk, 12 Sep 2019](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D9%82%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B6-%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AF-%D8%AA%D8%B5%D9%84-200-%D9%85%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC)). That grouping suggests a category-13 PCA engineer would fall in the second bracket, though no source confirms it. The loan is worth about €13,250 at the official rate or €7,250 at the street rate. The 1,000,000 DZD subsidy alone equals 10 to 17 months of an entry-level civilian cyber salary. The 2019 amounts may have changed since. Today the MDN runs subsidised promotional housing (LPP) for its cadres and an AADL rent-to-own quota for NCOs and civilian staff. Units were handed over in 2026 in Sidi Bel Abbès, Tissemsilt, Misserghin (400 units) and Relizane (250). **Presidential decree 24-391 of 12 December 2024 created a Military Social Housing Office** ([Echorouk, 25 Apr 2026](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%B3%D9%83%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC)). Social services have also paid the last four AADL instalments for serving personnel on MDN lists ([Awras](https://www.awras.com/%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%85-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%81%D8%A6%D8%A9-%D9%85%D9%86-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%B3%D8%A8%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%8A%D8%B4-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A5%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%86/)).

Three smaller benefits complete the package, all from the statute. **Family allowances** are part of military pay and continue in full even during a suspension (Art. 76). Serving and retired personnel can use the army's **social services** (Art. 61). And a career officer whose request is accepted can take up to **12 months of reconversion leave on full pay and allowances** before returning to civilian life, and that year counts toward the pension (Art. 86) ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). For a cyber engineer, that is a paid year to earn certifications before changing career.

## Signing locks you in: no side income and a costly exit

The rule that matters most for a cyber engineer is Article 34. An officer gives all of their professional activity to their assigned duties, and "**il lui est interdit d'exercer une activité privée lucrative à quelque titre et de quelque nature que ce soit**". The only exception, in Article 27, is teaching, research or publishing, and only with the defence minister's authorisation ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). While you serve, there is no bug-bounty income, no consulting and no remote contract for a foreign client.

Leaving early is expensive. Article 82 says an officer trained at the MDN's expense cannot leave on their own request before completing **effective service of at least twice the length of their training** (the "période de rendement"). Even if the request is accepted, the officer "est astreint au remboursement de la totalité des frais consentis pour sa formation, **y compris les traitements perçus durant cette période**" (same source). For a sur-titre officer trained for about two years, that suggests **about four years** of service before you can even ask to leave. For the five-year bac route through EMP, it suggests about ten. The text does not say whether training years count toward the période de rendement, and no published figure for training costs exists.

After that period, leaving is still not your decision alone. You must give **six months' notice** (Art. 121). Your resignation needs acceptance and can be rejected as "contraire à l'intérêt du service" (Art. 122). Article 123 lists when a request counts as contrary to the service's interest. One case is an officer who has **not yet reached the age limit or service limit of their rank**, which on its face covers almost every serving officer. The others are an unfinished période de rendement, mobilisation, and missions for which the officer is judged necessary (same source). In practice, a guaranteed exit may come only at a rank limit or at the 25-year pension point. No data shows how often resignations are refused.

Other rules reach into your private life and your career after the army. Travel abroad needs **prior authorisation** (Art. 36), which affects foreign conferences and certification exams. Marriage needs written authorisation (Art. 33). Secrecy duties continue after you leave (Arts. 44–45). For **five years after leaving**, you may not work in any form for companies that do business with the MDN. The minister can waive this, but for officers dismissed on disciplinary grounds the ban is permanent (Art. 35) ([JO n°12, 2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)). That ban could exclude state telecoms and IT integrators with MDN contracts, which are natural employers for a former cyber officer. A further five-year ban covers party politics and running for office (Art. 30 bis) ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/les-militaires-interdits-de-toute-activite-politique-pendant-5-ans-apres-leur-depart-a-la-retraite/)). Two caveats apply. These articles are quoted from the 2006 text, and the research did not check whether the 2016, 2019 or 2021 amendments changed them. And the research found no PCA rules on repaying training or on outside work, so a PCA candidate should ask about both before signing.

## Civilian cyber pay catches up by year five and pulls ahead by year ten

Two national benchmarks help set the scale. The SNMG has been **24,000 DZD gross** since January 2026 ([TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/algerie-le-salaire-minimum-passe-a-24-000-da-effet-retroactif-des-le-1er-janvier/)). The latest official average wage survey (ONS, 2022) put average net pay at **61,300 DZD in the public sector**, **35,200 in the domestic private sector** and **114,100 in extractive industries** ([Algerie360/ONS](https://www.algerie360.com/salaires-le-secteur-public-en-algerie-remunere-mieux-que-le-prive/); [Algerie360](https://www.algerie360.com/quels-sont-les-secteurs-qui-payent-le-mieux-en-algerie/)). No official survey covers cybersecurity pay, so every civilian figure below is an estimate, mostly weighted towards Algiers.

| Career stage | Army officer (unofficial) | PCA engineer at the MDN | Public sector and state firms | Private employers | Freelance or remote, foreign clients |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry (0–2 years) | 70,000–100,000 (sous-lieutenant/lieutenant) | 49,000–59,500 gross | 55,000–90,000; HCN offer 74,000 base | 45,000–90,000 | €200–500 (beginner) = 55,000–138,000 at street rate |
| Early career (2–5 years) | 75,000–120,000 (lieutenant/capitaine) | ~50,000–60,000 plus seniority steps | Sonatrach and Sonelgaz engineers ~54,000–120,000 | Security analyst 70,000–120,000 | €500–1,500 = 138,000–414,000 at street rate |
| Senior (5–10+ years) | 80,000–150,000 (capitaine/commandant) | Near 60,000 unless promoted | No cyber-specific data | RSSI/CISO 150,000–300,000; confirmed IT cadre 120,000–300,000 | €1,000–3,000 = 276,000–828,000 at street rate (151,000–453,000 official) |
| Top | 140,000–220,000 (colonel) | 78,000–95,000 at the grade's top step | — | Senior consultant 200,000–400,000 | Unsupported claims of $2,000–6,000 |

**At entry, the army wins.** A new lieutenant on 75,000–100,000 DZD earns 3–4 times the SNMG. That is more than Glassdoor's average for a "Cyber Security Engineer" in Algiers, about **59,000 DZD** (range 47,000–70,000, sample size unknown) ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.fr/Salaires/algiers-alg%C3%A9rie-cyber-security-engineer-salaire-SRCH_IL.0,15_IM960_KO16,39.htm)). It is level with or above the HCN's 74,000 DZD offer, and well above PCA. Big state firms report similar entry pay. Sonatrach engineers in Algiers report 70,000–120,000 DZD with a median near 80,000, and 65,000–86,000 in their first three years ([Glassdoor, Sonatrach](https://www.glassdoor.fr/Salaire-mensuel/Sonatrach-Informaticien-Alger-Salaire-mensuel-EJI_IE8552.0,9_KO10,23_IL.24,29_IM960.htm)). Sonelgaz "ingénieurs d'études" report 64,000–102,000 across 17 salaries ([Glassdoor, Sonelgaz](https://www.glassdoor.fr/Salaire-mensuel/Sonelgaz-Ing%C3%A9nieur-Alger-Salaire-mensuel-EJI_IE472846.0,8_KO9,18_IL.19,24_IM960.htm)).

**By year five, private employers catch up, and by year ten they pull ahead.** An Algerian IT forum's pay grid puts a security analyst with 2–5 years' experience at **70,000–120,000 DZD**, about a capitaine's pay. It puts an RSSI (head of information security) with 5–10 years at **150,000–300,000 DZD**, and a senior consultant at 200,000–400,000 ([ITTalents DZ, Dec 2024](https://itprenariat.dz/ittalentsdz/forum/topic/211)). A 2026 employment guide gives an experienced IT cadre, with cybersecurity named as in demand, **120,000–300,000 DZD** ([Sakina DZ, Mar 2026](https://www.sakinadz.com/blog/guides/travailler-algerie-guide-emploi-2026)). At the top of the range, a private RSSI earns up to twice a commandant's pay. One 2026 article claims senior cyber engineers in Algiers earn "DZD 400,000+" and that cyber pays 110% above the tech average, but it shows no data to support either claim ([AlgeriaTech News, Jun 2026](https://algeriatech.news/algeria-cybersecurity-engineer-salary-110-percent-algiers-talent-market-2026/)). These upper bands come from banks, telecoms, multinationals and hydrocarbons. The ONS private-sector average of 35,200 DZD shows that most private jobs pay far less.

**Foreign clients pay the most, and the army rules them out.** Algerian developers working through Upwork or Malt are said to charge **€80–200 a day**, with earnings of "2–4×" local salaries, or 100,000–400,000 DZD a month ([CTA DZ, Jun 2026](https://ctadz.org/salaire-developpeur-web-algerie-2026/)). Bloggers put a good freelancer with foreign clients at **€1,000–3,000 a month**, an intermediate one at €500–1,500 and a beginner at €200–500 ([ZoomAlgérie](https://zoomalgerie.com/salaire-developpeur-web-algerie/)). These figures are for developers, not cyber specialists. They come from blogs and leave out months without clients, platform fees and tax. The legal side also matters. Foreign-currency income must be declared and converted at the official rate, and most freelancers change euros on the parallel market, which is tolerated but not formally legal ([ZoomAlgérie](https://zoomalgerie.com/creer-business-en-ligne-algerie/)). Auto-entrepreneur status caps turnover at 15 million DZD a year ([Sakina DZ](https://www.sakinadz.com/blog/guides/travailler-algerie-guide-emploi-2026)). None of this is open to a serving officer (Art. 34).

The overall picture favours the army for one kind of person and not another. Suppose your realistic alternative is a salaried domestic job and you value stability. Then the army gives you the best starting pay available to a new graduate. On top of that come lifetime family healthcare, a pension at about 49 and subsidised housing, which no civilian employer in these sources matches. Now suppose you expect to become a top-quartile security specialist or to work for foreign clients. Then the army costs you real money, and most of the cost comes after year five, just when the période de rendement and the resignation rules make it hardest to leave.

## Conclusion

The army does not pay for cyber skills. It pays for rank, years of service and posting. In Algeria, the market premium for security expertise exists only in the civilian sector, and the military statute stops you from capturing it while you serve. So the choice is not between a salary of X and a salary of Y. It is between a secure floor (healthcare, an early pension, housing) and the open-ended upside you give up for at least the période de rendement, and possibly until a rank limit. The exchange rate shapes that upside more than most comparisons admit. At the official rate, €1,000 is about 151,000 DZD. At the street rate it is about 276,000. So roughly **45% of a freelancer's apparent dinar advantage comes from the parallel-market premium**, which is legally grey and could narrow. Measured at the official rate, a good freelancer earns about 1.5–6 times a new lieutenant, not 3–11 times.

Two numbers would change this analysis, and you can get both before signing. The first is a recent **fiche de solde** for a newly commissioned lieutenant in transmissions or informatique. It would settle the base-versus-allowance question and the engineer-premium question that no public source answers. The second is a **written statement of the période de rendement and the training-repayment amount** for your exact training route. That converts the lock-in from an abstract risk into a sum in dinars. Ask the recruitment office for both. Then watch the 2027 pay revision: Article 52's extension clause means army pay should rise with civil-service pay, which keeps the army's entry-level lead intact. PCA, by contrast, pays less than the Presidency's own HCN offer for the same diploma. Choose it for the access, the mission and the healthcare, not for the salary.
