## 1. What do cybersecurity / IT security engineers earn in Algeria outside the army: (a) the civilian public sector, (b) private companies and multinationals, (c) remote or freelance work for foreign clients?

### Takeaway
No official survey of cybersecurity pay exists. The best available estimates:
- **Public sector and state companies:** an entry-level state-qualified engineer (ingénieur d'État) earns roughly **55,000–90,000 DZD**. A real 2024 public posting offered **74,000 DZD base**.
- **Private employers:** security analysts with 2–5 years' experience earn about **70,000–120,000 DZD**. Experienced security managers (RSSI) earn about **150,000–300,000 DZD**. Some 2026 blog estimates put senior people at 400,000 DZD or more in Algiers, without data behind them.
- **Remote or freelance work for foreign clients:** the commonly cited range is about **€1,000–3,000 per month** (about 276,000–828,000 DZD at the parallel rate). These figures come from blogs and are weakly sourced.

### Inferences
- **A realistic civilian ladder for a cyber or IT-security engineer, in net DZD/month:**

  | Stage | Public sector / state companies | Private / multinational |
  |---|---|---|
  | Entry (0–2 yrs) | ~55,000–90,000 | ~45,000–90,000 |
  | Mid (2–5 yrs) | – | ~70,000–150,000 |
  | Senior or RSSI (5–10+ yrs) | – | ~150,000–300,000 |
  | Top of market (banks, telecom, multinationals, hydrocarbons; weakly sourced) | – | ~300,000–400,000+ |

- **Entry pay is close across sectors.** The ONS public-sector average (61,300 DA, 2022) and the HCN offer (74,000 DA base, 2024) suggest that public employers do not pay much less than local private firms at entry level. The private premium appears mostly at mid and senior levels.
- **Remote work for euro clients is the highest-paying path.** €1,000–3,000/month equals about **151,000–453,000 DZD** at the official rate, or **276,000–828,000 DZD** at the parallel rate. Twelve to fifteen billable days at €80–200 gives roughly €1,000–3,000/month, which matches the blog bands. That is about 2–6× a local mid-level salary.
- **The remote figures come from blogs and are optimistic.** They exclude periods without clients, platform fees and tax.
- **There is no reliable cyber-specific sample.** The low Glassdoor "Cyber Security Engineer" figure (~59,000 DZD) likely reflects junior or public-company respondents. Blogs that claim 400,000+ DZD are not backed by data.

### Gaps
- No specific salary data was found for **Algérie Télécom, Mobilis, the banks (BNA, BEA, CPA, BADR), CERIST, or the ministries** for IT-security posts. I also found no published collective-agreement grid for these employers.
- The category-13 ingénieur d'État index under Decree 23-54 and the current index-point value were not retrieved, so I cannot give an exact civil-service base salary.
- Emploitic listings for RSSI and security posts found in search **show no salaries**. Emploitic's own salary-data product was not found.
- I found no salary pages from Paylab or SalaryExplorer specific to cybersecurity in Algeria.
- No survey-based data on Algerian remote or freelance cyber earnings (bug bounty, pentest, SOC) was found. All remote figures are for developers and come from blogs.
- Most Glassdoor pages could not be fetched (HTTP 403), so their sample sizes and update dates are unverified.

## 2. What are the national minimum wage (SNMG) and average wage in 2025–2026, and the current DZD/EUR exchange rates (official and parallel)?

### Takeaway
- **Minimum wage:** the SNMG has been **24,000 DA gross/month since 1 January 2026**, up from 20,000 DA.
- **Average wage:** the latest ONS average net wage found is **43,500 DA (2022)**: 61,300 DA in the public sector and 35,200 DA in the private sector.
- **Exchange rates, mid-November 2026:** the official euro rate is about **151–152 DZD**. The parallel (Square) rate is about **275–277 DZD**, which is roughly 80% more.

### Cited Findings
- **SNMG decision:** The Council of Ministers of 30 November 2025 raised the SNMG from 20,000 to 24,000 DA from January 2026. — [Radio Algérie](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/fr/node/75060); [Radio Algérie (EN)](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/en/node/75102)
- **SNMG decree:** Presidential Decree 26-01 of 7 January 2026 made the increase official. It took effect on 14 January, with retroactive effect from 1 January. The SNMG is a gross amount and applies to both the public and private sectors. — [TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/algerie-le-salaire-minimum-passe-a-24-000-da-effet-retroactif-des-le-1er-janvier/); [Algerie360](https://www.algerie360.com/salaire-minimum-la-hausse-du-a-24-000-da-officiellement-entree-en-vigueur/)
- **Unemployment allowance:** It rose from 15,000 to 18,000 DA at the same time. — [Algérie Eco, 30 Nov 2025](https://algerie-eco.com/2025/11/30/algerie-le-snmg-passe-a-24-000-dinars-et-de-lallocation-chomage-a-18-000-dinars/)
- **Pensioners:** About 80% of retirees received a pension equal to the old SNMG (20,000 DA). — [Observ'Algérie](https://observalgerie.com/2025/12/02/societe/hausse-du-snmg-en-algerie-qui-va-vraiment-en-beneficier-et-comment/) (via search summary)

  - **43,500 DA overall:** public sector **61,300 DA**, domestic private sector **35,200 DA**.

  — [Observ'Algérie, 16 Nov 2026](https://observalgerie.com/2026/11/16/economie/taux-de-change-en-algerie-leuro-baisse-face-au-dinar-voici-les-cours-officiel-et-parallele/)

### Inferences
- **SNMG in euros:** 24,000 DA is about **€159 at the official rate** and about **€87 at the parallel rate**.
- **Average wage in euros:** the 2022 ONS average (43,500 DA) is about €288 official / €158 parallel.
- **Cyber pay relative to these benchmarks:** an entry cyber engineer on about 60,000–90,000 DZD earns roughly **2.5–3.75× the SNMG** and about 1.4–2× the national average wage.
- **Which rate to use:** for people paid in dinars who want to buy imported goods or travel, the **parallel rate** is the realistic measure of purchasing power. For remote workers paid in euros, the gap between the two rates is the main source of their dinar-terms advantage.

### Gaps
- **No ONS average wage for 2023, 2024 or 2025** was found. The 2022 survey is the latest located.
- **USD/DZD rates** (official and parallel) for November 2026 were not captured, so USD-quoted remote figures are not converted here.
- Several Algerie360 rate articles for "24–30 November" carry no year and show about 156 DZD/€. They probably belong to another year, so I did not use them.

## 3. What non-salary benefits do ANP officers receive: housing, free military healthcare, retirement, family allowances, leave, loans, social services, training?

### Takeaway
The legal basis is the military statute (**Ordonnance 06-02 of 28 Feb 2006**) and the military pensions code (**Ordonnance 76-106**, amended in 2021). Under them, officers and their dependants get:
- **free care in military hospitals**, for life, including in retirement;
- access to the army's **social services** (œuvres sociales);
- family allowances, paid leave, and a **12-month fully paid "reconversion leave"** before returning to civilian life;
- an **immediate pension after 25 years of service**, accruing at 3% per year (capped at 90%) on reference pay that includes allowances.

Housing support from the social services directorate is substantial. Reported examples: interest-free loans of **2,000,000 DA for officers** (2019 scheme), dedicated AADL and promotional-housing quotas (2026), and a new **Military Social Housing Office** created in December 2024.

### Cited Findings

**Pay structure and benefits in kind (statute)**
- **Art. 52:** Pay is a *solde* set by rank and seniority, **plus allowances** tied to the function, its constraints, the posting location and its risks. The officer "may benefit from **benefits in kind (prestations en nature)** fixed by regulation". Any general measure affecting civil servants' pay is extended to military personnel. — [Ordonnance 06-02, JORA n°12, 1 Mar 2006 (official text, joradp.dz)](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 76:** Family allowances (*allocations familiales*) form part of military pay. They are "maintained in full" even when an officer is suspended and receives only half pay. — [Ordonnance 06-02, JORA n°12/2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)

**Healthcare**
- **Art. 55 of the statute:** Active officers in any statutory position, **retired officers, and their dependants (ayants droit)** are entitled to access to, and **free care in, the hospitals of the military health service**, under conditions set by regulation. Service-related injury or illness is compensated under the military pensions code. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Private clinics (July 2012):**
  - The MDN social services directorate signed agreements with private clinics, dialysis centres and prosthetics centres.
  - Active and contract ANP personnel, civilian MDN staff **and their families** get treatment across specialties, with **100% of costs** paid by the **Military Social Security and Reserve Fund** (الصندوق العسكري للضمان الاجتماعي والاحتياط).
  - Coverage includes surgery, hospitalisation, lab tests, radiology, dental care and prostheses. Named clinics are in Algiers, Chlef, Oran and Annaba.
  - This is an old article; I did not confirm that the arrangement is still current.

  — [Echorouk, 2012](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D9%85%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1-%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%85-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AF)
- **Aïn Naâdja:** A later agreement with private clinics also covered retirees insured with the army's health scheme. Its stated aim was to relieve pressure on public hospitals, **especially the Aïn Naâdja military hospital (HCA)**. — [Ennahar](https://www.ennaharonline.com/%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D9%85%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%8A%D9%80%D8%B4-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%80%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AA/) (via search summary; date not confirmed)
- **Local facilities (April 2026):** A social medical centre and a kindergarten were inaugurated in Aïn Témouchent (2nd Military Region). — [Echorouk, 25 Apr 2026](https://www.echoroukonline.com/%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%AC-%D8%B3%D9%83%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC)

**Social services**
- **Art. 61 of the statute:** Active and retired military personnel benefit from the army's social services (*œuvres sociales*). — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)

**Leave**
- **Art. 49:** Besides legal leave (*congés légaux*), officers get **paid permissions** under regulation, but can be recalled immediately. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 86, reconversion leave:** A career officer may, on accepted request, take **reconversion leave of up to 12 months on full pay, including allowances**, to prepare a return to civilian life. The time counts toward the pension. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)

**Retirement (military pensions code, Ordonnance 76-106 as amended, text formatted 26 May 2024)**
- **Service thresholds:**
  - The pension right needs at least **15 years** of service.
  - Active officers who have **not completed 25 years** can retire only on an **approved request**.
  - Officers discharged after **25 years**, or for age limit, post abolition or service-related illness, get an **immediate pension**.
- **How the pension is calculated:**
  - It accrues at **3% per year** of service in the ANP (3.5% for ALN/FLN service), capped at **90%**.
  - The reference pay ("émoluments de base") is **gross pay tied to rank and index, including the highest allowances received during the last five years**.
  - The lowest pensions cannot be below the SNMG.
- **Survivors and death grant:** A surviving spouse gets **70%** of the pension (the page text is partly garbled) and each minor orphan **10%**. The death grant is **12× the monthly pension**.

  — [Code des pensions militaires (Ordonnance 76-106), consolidated text, ORA Constantine](https://oraconstantine.com/laws/%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9.php)
- **2021 amendment (Ordonnance 21-04):**
  - Kept the 15-year minimum.
  - **Aligned career NCOs with officers at 25 years** of actual service, to reduce the military pension fund's deficit.
  - Extended contract NCOs to 19 years; rank and file stayed at 15 years.
  - Extended the **separation grant** (منحة الصرف من الخدمة), with officers needing **8 years** of service.

  — [Radio Algérie, 20 Apr 2021](https://radioalgerie.dz/news/ar/article/20210420/210380.html); [El Maouid, 26 Mar 2021](https://elmaouid.dz/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%91%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%B6/); [El Youm, 20 Oct 2021](https://elyoum.dz/2021/10/20/%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B3%D9%83%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A3/)
- **End-of-service grant proposal (2012, not confirmed enacted):** An FLN parliamentary amendment proposed a grant of **one month's pay per year of service** for all ranks. — [Ennahar, 11 Dec 2012](https://www.ennaharonline.com/%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8-%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D9%86-%D9%83%D9%84-%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%8A%D8%B4-%D9%83%D8%AA/)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative pension:** an officer-engineer commissioned at about 23–25 could leave with an **immediate pension after 25 years, around age 48–50**. The pension would be about **75% of reference pay** (25 × 3%), and reference pay includes allowances. Serving 30 years gives about 90%. A civilian engineer could not get a pension at that age.
- **Lifetime healthcare is a large non-cash benefit:** free care for the officer, the spouse and children, extended into retirement by Art. 55. In a country where private care is paid out of pocket, this is worth a lot. I found no monetary valuation.
- **Housing is probably the largest single non-cash benefit:** interest-free loans of 2,000,000 DA, subsidies of 1,000,000 DA, and priority quotas for LPP and AADL. For comparison, 2,000,000 DA interest-free over 12 years saves the interest a civilian would pay on a bank loan, and the subsidy alone equals about **10–17 months of an entry civilian cyber salary** (at 60,000–100,000 DZD/month). The 2019 amounts may have changed since.
- **The 12-month fully paid reconversion leave** effectively gives up to a year's paid runway to set up a civilian career, for example obtaining certifications, before leaving.

Art. 81 refers the status of trainee officers to a regulation I did not find.
- **Training abroad:** no source found on frequency or conditions.
- **Logement de fonction (on-base service housing):** no specific details found for officers.
- **mdn.dz** returned **HTTP 503** again, so official MDN social-services pages could not be consulted.

I quoted the 2006 original for Articles 34, 49, 52, 55, 61, 82, 86 and 121–123 and did not verify whether these were later changed.
- **Unsettled figures:** the exact current officer age and service limits (2006 vs 2021 tables), and whether the 2012 one-month-per-year end-of-service grant was ever enacted.

## 4. What constraints affect an officer's earning power: minimum service commitment, restrictions on leaving early (resignation, repayment of training costs), ban on second jobs or freelancing, travel restrictions?

### Takeaway
- **No outside paid work:** the statute flatly **bans any private lucrative activity** while in service. The only exception is teaching, research or publishing with the defence minister's authorisation. Freelancing or remote work for foreign clients is therefore excluded while serving.
- **Leaving early is hard:**
  - An officer trained at MDN expense must serve a **"période de rendement" equal to twice the training duration**.
  - Leaving earlier, if allowed at all, requires **repaying all training costs, including the salaries received during training**.
  - A resignation request is deemed contrary to the interest of the service, and can be refused, whenever the officer has not reached the age or service limit of their rank.
- **Further restrictions:**
  - Travel abroad needs **prior authorisation**.
  - Marriage needs written authorisation.
  - After leaving, there is a **5-year ban on working for firms that have dealings with the MDN**, and a 5-year ban on political activity.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 34, ban on outside paid work:** An active officer "devotes the whole of his professional activity to the tasks assigned". Subject to Art. 27, "**il lui est interdit d'exercer une activité privée lucrative à quelque titre et de quelque nature que ce soit**". Gifts, inheritances and legacies must be declared. — [Ordonnance 06-02, JORA n°12/2006](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 27, the only exception:** With the **defence minister's authorisation**, an officer may teach or do scientific research for other bodies and publish scientific, literary or artistic works. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 82, minimum service and repayment:** An officer trained at MDN expense **cannot leave on their own request before completing effective service of at least twice the training duration** (the "période de rendement"). If the request is accepted, the officer "**est astreint au remboursement de la totalité des frais consentis pour sa formation, y compris les traitements perçus durant cette période**". — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 121, notice:** A request to leave service or to claim retirement must be filed **at least 6 months** before the effective date. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 122, resignation needs acceptance:** A career officer can leave on an **accepted request**, and the request "may be rejected if judged contrary to the interest of the service". — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 123, when a request counts as contrary to the service's interest:**
  - the officer has **not reached the age limit in rank or the service-duration limit**;
  - a trained officer has not completed the période de rendement;
  - mobilisation or war;
  - in peacetime, the officer is or will be engaged in missions where they are "judged necessary".

  — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 36, travel:** Movement inside Algeria must not hinder duties. **Travel outside the national territory requires prior authorisation** under regulation. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 35, post-service employment:** A career officer who leaves may not work "in any form" for **5 years in companies that have dealings with the Ministry of National Defence**. The minister can waive this and must inform the President. For officers dismissed on disciplinary grounds the ban is **total and permanent**. — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Art. 30 bis, post-service politics:** It imposes a **5-year ban on partisan political activity or running for elected office** after leaving. TSA dates the article to a 2016 amendment. A search summary of El Wassat attributes it to a law published in the Journal Officiel on 11 Dec 2019. I did not reconcile the two dates. — [TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/les-militaires-interdits-de-toute-activite-politique-pendant-5-ans-apres-leur-depart-a-la-retraite/); [NN-Algeria, 2021](https://nn-algeria.dz/%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%88%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B1-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE/)
- **Other restrictions in the 2006 statute:**
  - **Art. 33:** marriage requires prior written authorisation, and the spouse's occupation must be declared.
  - **Art. 26:** public speaking or media appearances need authorisation.
  - **Art. 29:** no membership of parties or unions; other associations need authorisation.
  - **Art. 32:** strikes and collective claims are banned.
  - **Art. 38:** the officer must serve "at any time and in any place".
  - **Art. 44–45:** secrecy and discretion obligations continue after leaving.

  — [Ordonnance 06-02](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2006/F2006012.pdf)
- **Detachment to civilian administrations (July 2024):** A 2024 decree sets the conditions for detaching military personnel to public civilian administrations. — [Algérie Eco, 15 Jul 2024](https://algerie-eco.com/2024/07/15/detachement-des-personnels-militaires-aupres-des-administrations-civiles-publiques-les-conditions-fixees/) (headline via search; content not fetched)

### Inferences
- **Lock-in for a typical engineer-officer:** for a 5-year MDN engineering course (for example the École Militaire Polytechnique), Art. 82 would imply **about 10 years** of effective service before the officer may even ask to leave. The text is ambiguous on whether training years count toward that period.
- **Cost of leaving early:** the officer would owe repayment of the full training cost **plus all pay received during training**, a potentially large sum.
- **Even after that period, leaving is not guaranteed:** Art. 123 lets the hierarchy refuse any request from an officer below the age or service limit of their rank. In practice an exit may be possible only at the rank limits (for example captain at 38/18 years under the 2006 text, or 42/22 as reported for 2021) or at the 25-year pension point.
- **Opportunity cost of the side-work ban:** Art. 34 removes the main upside identified in section 1, foreign-client remote or freelance work at about €1,000–3,000/month. A serving cyber officer cannot do bug bounties, consulting or remote contracts.
- **Job-market limits after leaving:** the 5-year ban on working for MDN-linked firms (Art. 35) may exclude many defence-adjacent cybersecurity employers, such as state telecom and IT integrators with MDN contracts, unless the minister grants a waiver. The required travel authorisation also limits attending foreign conferences or certification exams abroad.
- **The strongest pull toward staying:** the 12-month paid reconversion leave and the 25-year immediate pension.

### Gaps
- I did not find the regulations implementing Art. 80–82: the exact training-cost amounts for MDN engineering schools, how "frais consentis" is calculated, or whether the période de rendement includes the training years.
- I did not confirm whether Articles 34, 35, 82 and 123 were changed by the 2016, 2019 or 2021 amendments. I quoted the 2006 Journal Officiel text, retrieved from joradp.dz; the MDN-hosted copy at mdn.dz was unreachable.
- I found no data on how often resignation requests are accepted or refused in practice, or on typical repayment amounts.
- I found no rule specifically on **post-service freelancing for foreign clients**. Beyond Art. 35 (MDN-linked firms) and the secrecy obligations (Art. 44–45), no other restriction was located.
